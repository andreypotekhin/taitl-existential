## Environment and workload

Amazon Corretto JDK 17.0.15; Maven 3.9.10; macOS 15.6.1 aarch64. One reporting thread; default heap settings, with
Surefire's benchmarks-profile --enable-preview argument. No instrumentation agent or explicit heap cap. Processor/core
count and RAM are unavailable in the restricted environment. No other build or benchmark ran concurrently with a sample.

Each fixture creates 10,000 distinct equal Night City Cat objects. Each object reports Read, RL, Write, and Access,
repeated once or eight times: 40,000 or 320,000 reports per transaction. Typed rules include an All check,
read/lock/write
checks, and read/lock/write intents. Repeated reports run immediate intents repeatedly but fold validation by
report/type/object identity. Exact callback assertions require 80,000 validation and 40,000 × repeats intent calls.
The general-rule variant adds an On invariant and On intent, requiring 120,000 validation and 80,000 × repeats intent
calls. Corrected callback counts are required; skipped general callbacks are never presented as a faster baseline.

Model construction, configuration, and setup begin/close are excluded. One complete successful warmup precedes measured
success, rejection/explicit rollback, and fresh recovery, in that fixed order. Recovery includes a rejected transaction
followed by a fresh successful one. For rejection, the first cat turns grey and four ordinary All failures are
collected;
other checks continue. State resets and deterministic close/assertions remain inside the workflow clock. Each scenario
is measured once per process; three independent JVMs provide descriptive samples, not percentiles or a confidence
interval.

Typed controls use exactly the same scenario order and harness flag in baseline and candidate. General-rule runs use
separate candidate JVMs that also execute typed variants before the general variant. Those mixed-run typed numbers are
excluded from the paired comparison because their warmup/GC order differs. Initial exploratory samples before the final
ordering correction are also excluded. No measurements or outputs have been reused from an earlier topic.

## Reproduction

Copy the recorded benchmark into a separate git-archive checkout of the baseline commit. From that checkout, run sample
1, 2, and 3 in separate Maven invocations:

    mvn -q -Pbenchmarks -Dtest=AccessEventBenchmark -Dbenchmark.baseline=true -Dbenchmark.sample=1 test

Run the identical command three times from the candidate checkout for paired typed controls. The baseline property only
selects typed-only harness scenarios; it does not switch the implementation. For the candidate general-rule variant:

    mvn -q -Pbenchmarks -Dtest=AccessEventBenchmark -Dbenchmark.sample=1 test

Repeat with sample=2 and sample=3. All nine final invocations exited zero, selected one benchmark test, and passed exact
callback and cleanup assertions. The general=true variant is not run against the defective baseline, which would fail
those assertions. Times use ms/workflow; allocations are per-thread megabytes (MB, 1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes) from
BenchmarkMemory/ThreadMXBean. Tables round to one decimal; percentage comparisons retain the original unrounded values.
On 2026-10-26 these units were converted for presentation without rerunning measurements. The recorded source manifest
identifies the measured harness; the later output-format-only change prints ms and MB for future runs. No numerical
performance gate or new production dependency is introduced.

## Interpretation and limits

- 1 repeat, success: candidate median time -13.7%; median allocated MB +1.5% versus baseline.
- 1 repeat, rejection-rollback: candidate median time -9.2%; median allocated MB +1.1% versus baseline.
- 1 repeat, fresh-recovery: candidate median time +0.2%; median allocated MB +3.1% versus baseline.
- 8 repeats, success: candidate median time +6.1%; median allocated MB +4.5% versus baseline.
- 8 repeats, rejection-rollback: candidate median time +10.2%; median allocated MB +4.5% versus baseline.
- 8 repeats, fresh-recovery: candidate median time +9.0%; median allocated MB +4.5% versus baseline.

The correction adds the missing general selection family and restores declaration order across matched intent families.
Typed-only controls therefore include additional authorization grouping/ordering work even though no new callbacks
participate. Repeated-report workloads expose that cost. Retain it as a measured optimization target: cache/reuse more
of the intent selection without relaxing conjunctive authorization or declaration order. The short fixed-order harness,
JIT, GC, and background/thermal state limit conclusions; overlapping ranges do not establish significance or a speed
gate.
General scenarios are a first valid baseline for behavior previously skipped, not a paired performance comparison.

Allocation totals count transient objects over the entire workflow and can exceed process heap. They are not retained
memory or a leak result. Every completed/rejected-and-rolled-back workflow asserts an empty runtime event owner, and
fresh recovery verifies reuse of the library with a new handle. Direct acceptance additionally retains managed views
and verifies guards and explicit cleanup. No forced GC, weak-reference collection, graph-size measurement, or
class-loader reclamation claim is made here. Existing managed-index resource evidence remains separately linked.
